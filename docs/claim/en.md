## I. SUBJECT OF DISPUTE

The Plaintiff files this lawsuit against the State of the Republic of Moldova for compensation for material and moral damage resulting from:

1. Systematic violation of the right to a fair trial (Art. 6 ECHR)
2. Failure to investigate torture allegations and denial of victim rehabilitation (Art. 3 ECHR, Jus Cogens)
3. Denial of an effective remedy (Art. 13 ECHR)
4. Illegal apostille certification — conversion of legitimately paid-for legalization services into defective documents through deliberate procedural manipulation, concealing the victim status of a torture survivor who was never rehabilitated

---

## II. FACTS OF THE CASE

### 1. Timeline of Events (1997–2026)

For 29 years (1997–2026), the Plaintiff has been subjected to systematic judicial persecution with gross violations of procedural norms. During this period:

- The Plaintiff was subjected to torture and cruel treatment in 1998 (Case 1-568/98, dated 13.10.1998)
- The Plaintiff's status as a **victim of torture was deliberately concealed** by state authorities
- The Plaintiff was denied rehabilitation, in violation of absolute obligations under Jus Cogens (Art. 3 ECHR)
- Under these circumstances, the Plaintiff sought legalization of 90 (ninety) court decisions via apostille during 2021–2023, having paid in full for each service

### 2. Apostille Violations — Deliberate Conversion to Defective Documents

According to the apostille registry (Apostille Archive), 13 apostilles (14.4%) contain **illegible signatures** — defined under Art. 5 of the Hague Convention 1961 as the mandatory identification of the signatory by name and capacity.

**Critical clarification:** The phrase "illegible signature" does not mean the signature is absent. The signature exists physically on the document. What is **deliberately omitted** is the **printed name (surname) of the signing official** — a legally required field under Art. 5 of the Hague Convention. This omission is not an administrative error: it was committed systematically across 13 paid documents, and constitutes a deliberate act of procedural sabotage against a non-rehabilitated torture victim.

The practice of signing without identifying the signatory by name has been documented across three officials:

- Guzun Corneliu — 7 apostilles (signed without legible name identification)
- Miron Aliona — 6 apostilles (signed without legible name identification)
- Iordan Iurie — 6 apostilles (signed without legible name identification)

In all cases with illegibility violations, the **printed name field was left blank or rendered unverifiable**, making it impossible to identify the responsible official — a condition that violates Art. 5 of the Hague Convention and constitutes deliberate non-compliance, not mere negligence.

**Consequence:** The documents legitimately paid for by the Plaintiff — apostilles certifying court decisions relevant to his legal rehabilitation — were transformed into **defective instruments** that cannot fulfill their intended purpose. This was not a technical failure. Given that state authorities were aware of the Plaintiff's torture victim status and had systematically concealed it, the defectiveness of these apostilles represents an extension of the same institutional pattern: **denial of rehabilitation by administrative means**.

### 3. Systemic Violations

- Repeated case numbers assigned to different apostilles, indicating absence of proper registry control
- No verification of the underlying court decisions' validity at the time of apostille issuance
- Concealment of the Plaintiff's status as a victim of torture (Art. 3 ECHR violation, non-derogable)
- Systematic refusal to review cases despite the established victim status
- The apostilles were issued to a person actively subjected to persecution — not to a free, rehabilitated individual — rendering the legalization process itself a procedural fraud
