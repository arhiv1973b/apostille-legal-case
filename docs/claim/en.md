## II. SUBJECT MATTER

This civil action concerns the systematic issuance of defective apostilles by the Republic of Moldova over a period of 29 years (1997–2026), in violation of:

1. The Hague Convention Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents (1961), Article 3 — authentication requirements
2. The Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969), Articles 53 and 64 — jus cogens peremptory norms
3. The European Convention on Human Rights, Articles 3, 6, and 13

---

## III. STATEMENT OF FACTS

### 3.1 Apostille Violations (1997–2026)

Between 1997 and 2026, the Republic of Moldova issued **90 apostilles** to the Claimant. Statistical analysis of these documents reveals:

- **13 apostilles (14.4%)** bear unclear or illegible official signatures in violation of Hague Convention Art. 3
- Multiple apostilles lack proper authentication seals as required by the Convention
- No corrective mechanism was ever offered or applied despite formal complaints

### 3.2 Constitutional Court Refusal — 09.02.2009

On 09 February 2009, the Claimant filed an application before the Constitutional Court of the Republic of Moldova. The Court refused to hear the application **on language grounds**, in direct violation of:

- ECHR Article 6 — Right to a fair trial
- ECHR Article 13 — Right to an effective remedy
- Vienna Convention Article 85 — Equal authenticity of treaty languages

This refusal represents a fundamental denial of access to justice and is void under jus cogens principles.

### 3.3 Ministry of Health Obstruction — 02.05.2019

On 2 May 2019, a formal request was submitted to the Ministry of Health of Moldova regarding documentation records. The Ministry failed to provide a substantive response, violating the Claimant's right to effective remedy under ECHR Article 13.

### 3.4 Toderița Case — 05.12.2019

A parallel proceeding (Toderița case, 05.12.2019) demonstrates the systemic, rather than isolated, nature of Moldova's apostille authentication failures. This corroborating evidence establishes a pattern of state behavior.

## IV. LEGAL BASIS

### 4.1 Hague Convention (1961) — Article 3

The sole formality that may be required to certify the authenticity of a signature, the capacity in which the person signing the document has acted, and where appropriate, the identity of the seal or stamp which it bears, is the addition of the certificate described in Article 4. Moldova's 14.4% defect rate constitutes a systematic violation of this obligation.

### 4.2 Vienna Convention — Article 53 (Jus Cogens)

A treaty (or governmental act) is void if, at the time of its conclusion or execution, it conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law. The authentication standards established under the Hague Convention have acquired the status of jus cogens norms. Moldova's defective apostilles are therefore **void ab initio**.

### 4.3 Vienna Convention — Article 64

If a new peremptory norm of general international law emerges, any existing act which is in conflict with that norm becomes void and terminates. This provision renders all non-compliant apostilles void from the date of their issuance.

### 4.4 ECHR Articles 3, 6, 13

- **Article 3** — The systematic denial of rights through invalid documents constitutes inhuman treatment within the meaning of this Article
- **Article 6** — The Constitutional Court refusal on language grounds (2009) constitutes a violation of the right to a fair trial
- **Article 13** — No effective domestic remedy has been made available for 29 years of violations
